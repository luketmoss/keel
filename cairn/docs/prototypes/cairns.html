cairn · content model

A photo and a campsite are the same thing: a cairn

Today a photo lands where its EXIF says. A campsite lands where you clicked. Everything else about them — a name, a date, a description, a position, belonging to a trip or not — is identical. This prototype collapses them into one kind — a cairn, the marker you leave on your own map — and lets two independent attributes carry the difference: does it have an image, and where did its position come from.

The second is provenance, not ownership. A photo's EXIF seeds its cairn's location and can always be overridden — and a photo whose location cannot be worked out never becomes a cairn at all.

What the prototype settles

A photo is not a peer of a campsite

Campsite, water, hut, viewpoint and summit answer what is this place. A photo answers what do I have of it. In one enum you cannot have a photo of a campsite — and three of the cairns on this map are exactly that.

two attributes, not one type

The icon wins the marker

Flip the toggle to image wins and watch Ellery Creek camp, Counts Point and Mount Sonder collapse into identical circles. The map stops telling you where you can camp. Choosing an icon is an authored act, the same as placing the pin; the image is content, not identity.

a corner badge carries "there's a photo here"

Location belongs to the cairn, not to the photo

EXIF seeds the position; it does not own it. Every cairn drags, including one that came in with GPS — phone GPS drifts, and a photo taken from a lookout is of something across the valley. Moving a cairn sets its source to placed, and that override is permanent.

interpolation may only move what is still interpolated

A photo with no GPS is never orphaned, and never rejected

Import resolves EXIF first, then #52's interpolation against an open trip's tracks. What is left does not become a cairn at all — it waits in the draft, where #81's rule already says nothing has been written anywhere. Placing it is what makes it a cairn.

no coordinate, no record — so no orphan

Two ways to make a cairn, differing only in whether a file came with it

Drop a photo, or right-click the map. Both produce the same record. In both, the gesture's context decides ownership — a trip open means it lands in the trip, nothing open means loose. Right-click stays undiscoverable and needs a real affordance the prototype fakes with a hint.

open — the create affordance is unresolved

The batch does not wait on the stragglers

Drop five, three resolve and save immediately, two queue. Skip cycles the queue, discard drops only what is still unplaced. A blocking dialog per photo would make a forty-photo import unusable, and making all forty wait on two would be worse.

a suggestion beats a blank map: nearest route cairn by time

The chip row is the real cost

Try all three. Cairns is honest but loses "show me just my photos". Split keeps that filter but files a photographed campsite under Photos, where you won't look for it. +icons is the most expressive and the most chrome.

open — split reads best at this data size

Retyping is free, and that is the tell

Open any photo and pick an icon. Wallaby Gap becomes a campsite without touching its image, its date or its position. Under a type enum that operation has to ask what happens to the photo; under two attributes there is nothing to ask.

the model change, made tangible
What this deliberately does not cover
  • The migration itself. Every existing loose photo record, every trip's photos.json, photoCount, PhotoLayer, PhotoList, positionPhotos and the /photos/:id route all move. That is the bulk of the work and none of it is visible here.
  • Adding a photo to a cairn that already exists. Dropping on the map makes a new cairn; dropping onto an open cairn should attach to it instead. Not built.
  • Photos already in Drive with no position. The draft rule covers new imports, but records that already exist cannot be un-imported. They need the same placement queue, run once at migration — the one place a saved cairn may legitimately be unplaced.
  • The suggestion is faked. It interpolates the route by date. The real one reads timedPointPool for the nearest point in time, including across the ten-minute gap #52 refuses to cross automatically.
  • What photoCount becomes. The add-to-trip picker's 4T · 128P has to mean "cairns carrying an image", which needs a definition it never needed before.
  • Export. A cairn with an icon is a KML Placemark and should round-trip to Google Earth, which is cheaper than it is for any other kind.
  • Mobile. Long-press is wired but the bottom sheet is not; the column here is the desktop form only.
