Forage × Thrive · prototype, before issues

Forage goes deep on food; Thrive lines it up with everything else

Exploring what you eat lives in Forage, on its own Trends screen opened from a button in Eaten's header. Eaten itself doesn't change. Thrive gets daily food totals as one more Trends group, so calories and protein sit on the same timeline as sleep, HRV, weight and training. Thrive never sees individual foods, and Forage never reads Thrive.

One rule for where a chart goes

Forage answers "what and how do I eat?" Anything that needs a food, a meal or a recipe stays there. Thrive answers "how does that line up with the rest of me?" Anything that needs a workout, sleep or the scale goes there, and it only needs daily food totals for that.

Data flows one way

Forage Sheet→Forage API→getFoodLogTotals, days only→Thrive
Thrive→Forage

This connection already exists. Thrive's Day card (#714) uses it, through a token Forage accepts for getFoodLogTotals only. Thrive's Food group reuses that call over a longer range, so there are no new permissions and no Apps Script change. If Forage showed training days, it would need its own way into Thrive, and the line would blur in both directions.

Drinks stay where they are

Water, coffee, alcohol and Unwind are logged by Thrive Sip and are habits Thrive tracks. They stay in Thrive's Intake group, and Food becomes the tab next to it. Forage doesn't show drinks.

Try it
  1. Use the Forage / Thrive switch above the phone, or pick a scene.
  2. In Forage, the phone opens on Eaten as it is today. Tap the outlined Trends button in its header.
  3. Tap any bar to read that day. Change the range at the top.

Forage: Eaten stays as it is, and Trends opens from it

Eaten doesn't change. It still opens on today. The ‹ › arrows still step back through earlier days, every meal card keeps its Add row, and adding to a past day works exactly as it does now. The only addition is one Trends button in the header, next to your account button.

Trends is its own screen (#/eaten/trends), with a back arrow. Back returns you to Eaten on the same day you left from. Trends doesn't add or edit anything.

From a chart back to a day: tap any bar and the readout offers Open day ›. On a gap it says Log this day ›, which takes you to that date in Eaten, ready to add. So the trends also show you which days you forgot to log.

Try it
  1. Tap ‹ to go back a few days, then tap + Add on a meal.
  2. Tap the Trends button, then Back: you're still on the same day.
  3. In Trends, tap a dashed gap, then Log this day ›.

It isn't a fifth tab: Trends reads the same log, and the bar keeps its four tabs. If cooking and shopping trends grow later, a Trends tab is the fallback.

Tabs inside Trends

  • Energy: daily kcal with a 7-day average, plus averages by weekday. The Day card's rules carry over: a day with nothing logged is a gap, never 0, a partial day is hatched, and kcal are whole numbers.
  • Macros: the share of calories from protein, carbs and fat (4/4/9), how that split changes week to week, and grams per day for one macro at a time.
  • Nutrients: the seven, each with an average and a sparkline. Tap a row for its chart. Values read "at least" when some entries didn't report them.
  • Meals and Foods: next scene.

No goals yet

As in #714, there are no target lines, because nobody has chosen targets. The averages are relative to your own history. Whether to add goals is a question for the end.

Where the data comes from

Energy, Macros, Nutrients and Meals all come from getFoodLogTotals with by_meal, which the Forage app can already call. They need no API change.

Meals and foods: depth only Forage has

Two more tabs on the same Trends screen. Eaten is still untouched.

  • Meals: how a typical day splits across Breakfast, Lunch, Dinner, After dinner and Snacks. Each slot shows its average on the days it was eaten and how often that was. "After dinner, 310 kcal on 14 of 27 days" is the kind of thing you'd want to know.
  • Foods, in three parts:
    • The foods that contribute most to a metric (calories, protein, sodium, added sugars) over the range, with their share and how often they were logged. A logged recipe ranks as itself.
    • Your usuals: the usuals added most often in the range (#876), each counted once per time it was added.
    • Most cooked: from cook history, with ratings.

The one new read

Foods needs entries, not totals. getFoodLog over the range already returns them, with group_id and usual_id, so the app can count foods, recipes and usuals itself. An API action is only needed if a year of entries turns out to be slow. Cook history is getCooks.

Thrive: a Food group in Trends

A new group, Food, after Intake: Calories, Protein, Carbs, Fat, drawn like every other Trends card, with the same range and average controls. All four are added to the Custom picker under a Food section.

  • Gaps, not zeros. Thrive's drink metrics fill a missing day with 0, which is right for drinks. For food it would be wrong: not logging isn't the same as not eating. Food days break the line.
  • Each card ends with Explore in Forage, which opens Forage's Trends on the same metric and range.
  • No fiber, sodium or other nutrients here. That depth stays in Forage.

Optional: fuel and training

One card that only Thrive can draw: average calories on ride days, lift days and rest days, from Forage totals and Thrive's activities. It's marked optional in the phone, so it's your call whether it belongs here or in the weekly brief.

Side by side is where correlation happens

Custom already stacks up to four metrics on one time axis, and tapping a day reads all of them at once. With Food in the picker, Protein, Training Load, HRV and Weight line up with no new screen. That's the food-and-activity view you were after.

  • New, optional: a "Shade ride days" switch, which draws the days with a ride as bands behind every chart. It helps the eye find patterns without the app claiming any.
  • The screen shows the data and doesn't conclude anything. Graded conclusions stay with the nightly brief and the wiki's tests (#830), such as test-calories-weight-trend and test-protein-next-lift.
